Hello!

Discord is rumored to be up for an [initial public offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_public_offering), and following tradition, the company is doing everything it can to
make their brand new shares look tantalizing to people who believe owning things is a contribution to society. The details are a little muddy at this 
point since their response to backlash has been damage control claims about who will need to verify and to delay the rollout until the 
[second half of 2026](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/38332670254231-Age-Assurance-Update-FAQ).

{{ figure(img_path = "/posts/post2/delay.png",alt_text="A screenshot of Discord's age verification faq page informing of the rollout delay.",caption="I'm still not sure how age verification is going to do anything to stop adults from entering spaces meant for minors but okay :)") }}


Lets look at some of the claims on the age assurance FAQ page shall we?

> "Most users never access age-restricted content or change their default safety settings. For those who do, we have an internal system that determines your age using account-level signals — like how long your account has existed, whether you have a payment method on file, and general patterns of account activity. It does not read your messages, analyze your conversations, or look at the content you post."

I don't think the existence of this system is much better. While under no circumstances do you "gotta hand it to em," let's assume we can trust Discord now and in the future with not selling or using our data for any other purpose. 
There's a metaphor I would like to introduce you to: __Data is radioactive.__ If you decide to use it, even in a seemingly insignificant way, you assume a massive responsibility when it comes to storing and disposing of it.
Consider the story of a French power company that decided to upgrade their customers' power meters to internet enabled devices to collect electricity usage statistics and inadvertently created
a [registry of every muslim household under their service.](https://www.edps.europa.eu/data-protection/our-work/publications/techdispatch/techdispatch-2-smart-meters-smart-homes_en#:~:text=However,2015%29%2E)

I don't trust companies with this data. Even if policies are reasonable there's no guarantee they will continue to be in the future, and there's no guarantee 
your information won't end up exposed in a [breach.](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/02/discord-faces-backlash-over-age-checks-after-data-breach-exposed-70000-ids/)

> "If you're among the less than 10% of users who do need to verify, we'll give you options — designed to tell us only your age and never your identity."

> "We're adding more verification options."
